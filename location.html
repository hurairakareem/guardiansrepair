About Canton, IL

Canton is a historic community in Fulton County, Illinois, located in west-central Illinois. The city has a long industrial and agricultural heritage and has grown into an important hub within the surrounding county.

Canton was founded in 1825 by settler Isaac Swan, and its history is closely tied to manufacturing, agriculture, education, and healthcare.

Illinois Route 9 and Illinois Route 78 intersect in the downtown area, connecting Canton to Farmington to the north and the surrounding county to the east and west. Canton is also home to Spoon River College.

A historic Illinois community

Nearly two centuries of history

Canton's history dates back almost 200 years, built on agriculture, manufacturing, and entrepreneurship — including early plow and agricultural equipment manufacturing.

The city's industrial history includes the Parlin & Orendorff Company, which later became part of International Harvester's Canton Works. Today, Canton combines that historic character with modern healthcare, education, and local business.

Canton & Fulton County

Part of a larger region

Canton is the largest city in Fulton County, which covers about 883 square miles in west-central Illinois, between the Mississippi and Illinois Rivers — with the Illinois River forming part of the county's southeastern boundary.

The surrounding area includes rolling farmland, wooded areas, and waterways, with corn, soybeans, and livestock among the region's agricultural activities. Fulton County is also known locally as “Spoon River Country,” a nod to the river running through it.

Parks & downtown

More than 1,400 acres of parks

Canton is known for its public park system — over 1,400 acres of open park space across the city, alongside a historic downtown, local businesses, and community amenities.

The downtown district reflects the city's history through its historic buildings and central city park, and remains an important part of Canton's local identity today.
