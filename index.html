Explore by topic

A resource for understanding your garage door.

Straightforward information on the most common garage door topics — each one links to a full page with more detail.

Garage Door Repair

Doors usually signal trouble before they fail outright — grinding noises, slow response, or a door that won't fully close are common early warning signs. Left alone, these issues can turn into safety hazards, especially around springs and cables under tension. If your door is behaving oddly, it's worth a look before a minor issue becomes an emergency.

Learn more about Garage Door Repair →

Garage Door Springs

Torsion springs mount above the door and are common in newer homes; extension springs run along the upper tracks and are more typical in older installations. Both wear out over time, usually showing up as a visible gap in the coil, added noise, or a door that suddenly feels heavier to lift. Because springs store high tension, replacing one isn't a safe do-it-yourself project.

Learn more about Garage Door Springs →

Garage Door Openers

Belt-drive openers run quieter and suit garages attached to living space, while chain-drive openers tend to be more affordable and durable for detached garages. Many newer openers add smart, Wi-Fi-connected features for remote control and monitoring. Common opener issues include worn gears, misaligned safety sensors, and remotes that stop pairing.

Learn more about Garage Door Openers →

Garage Door Maintenance

Routine maintenance — lubricating moving hardware, testing the auto-reverse safety feature, and inspecting rollers and cables for wear — catches small problems before they become expensive ones. Seasonal changes in temperature affect metal hardware and weather seals, so a check twice a year is a reasonable baseline for most homes.

Learn more about Garage Door Maintenance →

Garage Door Installation

Material choice affects both durability and energy efficiency: steel resists dents and warping, while insulated doors help regulate temperature in garages attached to living space. Style ranges from traditional raised-panel doors to flush, modern designs. Installation itself involves confirming the opening is square, mounting new track and hardware, and testing the opener and safety sensors.

Learn more about Garage Door Installation →

Commercial Garage Doors

Businesses typically choose between sectional overhead doors, roll-up doors, or dedicated loading dock equipment depending on traffic volume and available space. Warehouse and dock doors cycle far more often than residential doors, which makes preventive maintenance especially valuable for avoiding costly downtime.

Worth knowing

Why winter is the toughest season on a garage door.

Cold weather is genuinely harder on garage door hardware. Metal springs and cables contract in low temperatures, which adds extra strain right at the point they're already under the most tension — it's a real reason spring failures cluster in the colder months.

Ice and packed snow in the track can also throw a door off alignment, and stiff, cold weatherstripping seals less effectively than it does in warmer months. None of this means you need to panic every winter — it just means a door that's already a little worn is more likely to show it once temperatures drop.
