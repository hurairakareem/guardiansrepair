Spring Repair

Reliable garage door spring repair

Garage door springs play an important role in lifting and balancing your garage door. When a spring breaks, wears out, or loses tension, the door can become difficult or impossible to open.

Signs to watch for

Signs of a broken garage door spring

A damaged spring can cause several noticeable problems. Contact a garage door professional if:

  • Your garage door suddenly won't open
  • The door feels unusually heavy
  • The door opens only a few inches
  • You hear a loud bang from the garage
  • A visible spring appears broken or separated
  • The garage door closes too quickly
  • The door looks uneven when opening
  • Your automatic opener struggles to lift the door
Spring types

Torsion vs. extension springs

Torsion springs are commonly installed above the garage door opening, using stored mechanical energy to help balance and lift the door. When one breaks or wears out, the door can become heavy, unbalanced, or difficult to operate.

Extension springs are typically positioned along the sides of the door and extend as it operates. If one breaks or becomes damaged, the door may operate unevenly or become difficult to open.

Our service includes inspecting the spring system and replacing damaged components when appropriate, with the right replacement depending on your specific door system.

Getting it right

Garage door spring replacement

Springs are wear components and eventually require replacement. When replacing one, correct specifications matter. Factors can include:

  • Garage door weight
  • Door height and width
  • Spring type
  • Spring dimensions
  • Number of springs
  • Door configuration
  • Hardware and lifting system

Why it matters

Why spring problems shouldn't be ignored

A malfunctioning spring can affect more than just the door's ability to open. Continuing to operate a garage door with a damaged spring can place extra stress on the opener, cables, rollers, and tracks.

If you notice a broken spring or a sudden change in how your door operates, stop using the system if it seems unsafe and contact a qualified professional.

Repair vs. replacement

What usually needs to happen

Some spring systems may be adjustable or serviceable, but a physically broken or worn-out spring generally requires replacement. The right call depends on the condition, type, dimensions, and configuration of your specific spring system.

A professional inspection helps determine what actually needs to be replaced, rather than swapping unrelated components unnecessarily.

Common questions

What people ask before calling.

How do I know if my garage door spring is broken?

A door that suddenly becomes very heavy, won't open, or makes a loud bang may have a broken spring. A visible separation in the coil can also indicate failure.

Can I replace a garage door spring myself?

We don't recommend it. Springs store significant mechanical energy, and improper handling can result in serious injury or property damage.

How long do garage door springs last?

Lifespan varies depending on the spring type, door weight, usage frequency, installation, and other factors.

Should both garage door springs be replaced?

If your door uses two springs, the condition of both should be evaluated. Depending on age and condition, replacing both is sometimes recommended — it depends on the specific system.

Can a broken spring damage my garage door opener?

Operating an opener with a broken spring can place extra load on it, since the opener may be attempting to lift the full weight of the door. Avoid repeatedly operating a door with a suspected broken spring.
